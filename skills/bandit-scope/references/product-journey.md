# Connect planning, marketing, design, and the business model

Use this guidance when the requested decision crosses these stages, or for a
focused marketing, product-design, or business-model brief. Keep the requested
stages and depth; a pricing question does not require a launch strategy.
Use the existing plan as the shared source of decisions. These are planning
deliverables; a design brief is not a rendered or tested interface.

## Carry one offer through the stages

For a combined request, work through research → product scope → marketing →
design → business model, then reconcile the whole offer. Start with provisional
price and delivery constraints where needed; do not postpone affordability or
capacity until after making promises. Revisit earlier choices when later work
contradicts them. A completed section alone is not a completed handoff.

Keep the chosen audience, user/buyer/approver, problem, promised outcome,
eligibility, price unit, delivery limits, and evidence status consistent across
the artifacts. Link to existing decisions rather than silently creating new
terms at each stage. For a durable handoff, show the material connections:

| Evidence or assumption | Product choice | Public promise and channel | User journey | Revenue/cost implication | Next check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source and what remains uncertain | Included outcome and limits | Claim the offer can support | How that same outcome is reached | Who pays and what delivering it consumes | Observable result that could change the choice |

Use a short paragraph instead when it carries the same information clearly.
Mark adopted decisions, recommendations, and unresolved assumptions separately.

## Research the decision

Apply [research guidance](research.md): identify the consequential uncertainty,
inspect available customer behavior and alternatives, and preserve source,
date, audience, offer conditions, and contrary evidence. When fresh external
facts matter and browsing is available, actually retrieve first-party sources
and connect findings to the choice. Do not merely propose research when the
request includes doing it. When access is unavailable or forbidden, assess the
supplied material and name the unresolved claims.

Compare alternatives on the customer's task, switching cost, reach, and relevant
offer terms. A competitor's positioning is a claim; interviews, traffic, or a
free waitlist do not establish paid demand. Let the research change a segment,
promise, channel, design assumption, or economic assumption when warranted.

## Make marketing actionable

State the initial segment, situation, current alternative, offered benefit,
reason to believe it, and a meaningful difference the evidence can support.
When a marketing deliverable is requested, draft usable headline/supporting
copy and a call to action. Include consequential eligibility, price, timing,
or availability limits near the promise. Do not manufacture testimonials,
urgency, superiority, or measured benefits.

Choose an initial channel with a reason the team can reach this audience there.
State the audience/recruitment path, offer, material to prepare, responsible
role, time window, capacity and spending limit. Explain the strongest channel
alternative when it changes the decision. A list of channels is not a launch
plan. Keep consent and contact permissions within the supplied constraints.

Connect exposure → response → first value → payment or repeat use where relevant.
Define event, numerator, denominator, observation window, and collection method;
separate planned thresholds from observed results. State proceed/change/stop
and inconclusive conditions. Count acquisition spending and staff effort on an
explicit basis; a click cost is not a customer acquisition cost. Proposing copy
or an experiment does not authorize publishing, outreach, or spending.

## Hand a coherent design to the next contributor

Describe the core task from entry through the promised outcome, including an
offline or staff handoff if delivery depends on it. For the requested surfaces,
give screen/content order or a text wireframe, primary action, visible offer
terms, and feedback. Connect each material screen to its product rule and the
marketing promise it must fulfill. Reuse the product's design language when
provided; label a proposed visual direction when none exists.

Cover the meaningful empty, waiting, success, failure, unavailable, and recovery
states, without forcing irrelevant states into every task. Explain role changes
such as user → approver → payer. Keep price, billing unit, cancellation and
delivery conditions consistent at the point of commitment. Include relevant
mobile and accessibility needs (readable hierarchy, labeled controls, keyboard
use, and errors that do not rely on color alone).

State a realistic prototype task, what a participant must understand or complete,
and how success/confusion will be observed. A proposed usability check is not a
completed test. Provide enough content and behavior to build a prototype; select
a rendering or implementation tool only when that work is also authorized.

## Design a business model that can deliver the promise

Connect customer and payer, value proposition, acquisition and ongoing service,
charging unit and timing, delivery activities/resources/partners, and cost.
Compare plausible models on the same audience and value when a model choice is
requested; recommend one with its tradeoff and what would reopen it. A monthly
price by itself is not a business model. Keep price tests provisional where
willingness to pay has not been observed.

Show the calculations that bear on the decision, with inputs, sources or labeled
assumptions, currency, units, and period. Separate fixed costs, variable delivery
costs, acquisition costs, and staff time. For example, contribution per delivered
unit is its net revenue minus its attributable variable costs; do not call that
profit before fixed costs. Do not count deposits as earned revenue or count the
same payment, refund, acquisition cost, or labor twice. Explain accounting
boundaries when inputs are incomplete rather than inventing precision.

Before reporting numeric tables or budgets, use an available calculator or code
tool to recompute totals, fees, period conversions, and break-even from the
listed inputs. Reconcile allocated spending, reserves, and explicitly unallocated
funds with the budget; state unused capacity instead of making the total equal
the cap by assertion. If calculation tools are unavailable, show the arithmetic
and check the sum independently. A within-budget plan can still have a wrong total.

Verify currency identity before comparing money across sources: a shared `$`
symbol does not prove a shared currency. If a source leaves it unspecified,
label that price's currency unverified, keep any own-price arithmetic conditional,
and withhold cross-source monetary comparisons until currency and billing terms
are aligned. An unknown local checkout currency and an unknown quoted currency
are different gaps; name the one the source actually leaves open.

Use low/base/high assumptions or a sensitivity calculation when uncertainty
could reverse the recommendation. Compare break-even volume, if contribution
is positive, with realistically reachable demand AND fulfillment capacity.
Nonpositive contribution has no volume-based break-even under those assumptions.
Unknown retention cannot justify an observed lifetime value. Include the smallest
next test of the uncertain price, cost, acquisition, or repeat-use assumption.

## Reconcile and review

Before handing off, trace one eligible person from the actual message through
the design to delivery and payment, and one consequential failure/recovery case.
Check audience, promised benefit, offer/version, eligibility, price/unit,
delivery time, capacity, and cancellation terms across the supplied artifacts.
Recompute decision-changing figures and distinguish cash collected, delivered
value, refunds, contribution, and unmeasured repeat demand.

If a later price or capacity decision changes the offer, revise affected copy,
flow, scope, metrics, and acceptance scenarios together within the authorized
scope. Preserve prior observations with their original conditions. In a review,
leave artifacts unchanged and report source-located conflicts with corrections.
Finish with the requested artifacts, material unresolved choices, and who should
check what next. Coherence is not market validation or proof of implementation.
