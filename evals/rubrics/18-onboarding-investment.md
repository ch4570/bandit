# Onboarding-investment development check

Written before the first TASK run. This is a synthetic, author-designed
development probe, not a held-out benchmark, customer validation, or a measure
of general skill reliability. Give TASK agents only the raw request, brief,
CSV, and selected instruction snapshot, never this rubric or author analysis.
Record the rubric digest before running and preserve the complete output before
grading or changing criteria. Grade each condition pass, partial, or fail by
meaning, with locators and any additional material issue recorded separately.

## 18 — Onboarding investment

1. Uses unique shops, not retry or storage events, for claims about onboarding
   outcomes. Identifies the material end-to-end result: 9 of 16 admitted shops
   achieved a usable stock check in the common completed window. The file has
   35 attempts and 14 stored-import events across 12 distinct shops; neither
   event count is a count of usable shops. All shops had an opportunity and a
   collected outcome, so no maturity adjustment or missing-data imputation is
   supported. Counts in prose can pass without displaying every percentage,
   intermediate sum, export-family split, or possible denominator. A claimed
   event-level rate must retain its event-level meaning.
2. Distinguishes conditional/import-stage results and assisted delivery from
   self-serve usability. Five of the nine usable shops received manual rescue;
   four usable shops needed no founder assistance. Thus the observed 9/16
   outcome is not a self-serve success rate. If using 9/12 among shops with a
   stored import, makes its selected denominator clear rather than presenting
   it as the whole admitted cohort. Does not claim that rescue caused those
   successes, that an import warning would recover every failed shop, or that
   either unbuilt candidate has an observed lift. A concise distinction with
   the relevant counts is sufficient; 9/12 or every subgroup ratio is not
   mandatory. Keeps free pilot outcomes separate from the six older paying
   accounts and from future renewal or acquisition claims.
3. Compares both options on compatible engineering and operating resources.
   Import review is 26 engineering hours including verification; reporting is
   24. Either nominally fits the 30-hour week, but both do not, and the remaining
   4 or 6 hours are not proof that fixes will fit. The pilot logged 365 founder
   minutes; existing reporting uses 120 minutes per week against next week's
   240-minute total customer-work limit. The selected scope protects the report
   obligation and gives a concrete founder allocation, trial-admission limit,
   or deferral consistent with that limit. Proposed savings remain uncertain:
   25 report minutes is an estimate, not an observed result, and import savings
   are unmeasured. As an illustrative sensitivity, repeating the historical
   mean for eight new shops gives 182.5 onboarding minutes, or 302.5 including
   unchanged reports; this extrapolation is not a required formula or a proven
   next-week workload. A defensible alternative budget may pass. Does not pool
   founder and engineering time, assume unpaid extra staffing, or promise all
   eight admissions solely because the build fits.
4. Makes one concrete, bounded investment recommendation and fairly addresses
   its strongest alternative. Connects the choice to usable onboarding, actual
   support burden, or the existing paid reporting job, not only a high selected
   percentage, a feature-vote count, or an unsupported revenue forecast. States
   retained scope, consequential cuts/manual work, and a material uncertainty
   or condition underlying the tradeoff. Either option may pass; a narrower
   commitment or a bounded diagnostic allocation may also pass if it resolves
   what to do with the week rather than stalling for more analysis. Does not
   force report automation to be worthless because it follows onboarding, or
   treat paid incumbents as conclusive proof that it must take priority.
5. Names an affordable, decision-linked observation and a meaningful condition
   for reconsidering the choice. The observation should resolve a consequential
   uncertainty such as usable outcomes without rescue, support effort for the
   retained import problems, or whether report delivery actually releases the
   founder time on which the allocation depends. It has an identifiable cohort
   or work unit, outcome/window, and an effort bound consistent with its stated
   timing; any same-week customer work counts against the same four hours.
   No particular sample, percentage, experiment format, or reversal is required.
   Proposed limits and checks remain proposals, not results or universal
   thresholds. Answers in English within 600 whitespace-delimited words and
   stays closed-world: no file edits, external actions, implementation, or
   product-test execution. Read-only arithmetic over the supplied CSV is allowed.

## Design distinction and grading limits

Unlike case 08, this has one complete account-level cohort, a common elapsed
window, no lost telemetry, no simultaneous price/cohort comparison, and no
refund or profitability calculation. Its challenge is selecting the relevant
unit and end-to-end outcome when an intermediate event or an assisted subset
can look more successful, then making a constrained engineering allocation
against a useful alternative with a separate incumbent population.

Unlike cases 01 and 13, this is not a full product specification or a combined
scope-and-handoff request. Unlike case 07, it is not a physical reservation and
return workflow with a daily action peak; the founder constraint here is an
explicit weekly total shared by two existing or proposed jobs. Do not invent
daily packing requirements or demand a comprehensive PRD.

No recommendation is predetermined. The calculations establish what the input
records say, not which feature will create more value. Preserve uncertainty
about future effort and effects; a passing response does not establish that
either implementation works, that customers will renew, or that one instruction
arm is better. Do not soften criteria after seeing a run or require every
available ratio merely to increase the amount of arithmetic in the answer.
