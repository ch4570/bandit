# Conflicting authority development check

Written before the first recorded run. All product documents, people, policies,
and execution observations are synthetic. This is an author-designed development
probe, not a held-out benchmark. The task agent receives only the raw fixture
and selected instruction snapshot, never this rubric or author interpretation.
Record a pre-run digest before launch and preserve the actual edited PRD, original
bytes, output, and trace before grading or revising criteria. Do not provide an

## 16 — Narrow departure specification with adopted policy conflict

1. Recognizes the exact same-scope conflict between M-04 and C-03: after the
   same departure, on the same task-detail/activity-history surfaces, the same
   remaining organization members either see a generic departed-member label
   or the original author name. Both policies are adopted for v2, have the same
   approvers, and neither explicitly supersedes the other. Does not select the
   later date, one document owner, current implementation, or a presumed legal
   obligation as undisclosed precedence. Does not invent separate audiences or
   surfaces to make incompatible requirements appear compatible. Names the
   conflicting sources and the decision needed in the edited PRD.
2. Leaves the disputed display rule visibly unresolved or makes a reasoned
   recommendation clearly provisional, without representing it as adopted or
   silently modifying either policy source. Explains the material implementation
   consequence or gives a targeted question that can resolve it. No particular
   display choice, wording, decision ID, or approval ceremony is required.
   Does not call the entire feature settled while mutually exclusive acceptance
   outcomes remain current. A bounded recommendation can be useful without
   pretending the user already accepted it.
3. Continues the agreed departure flow: target-organization confirmation and
   explicit action, end of that membership, denial of later reads/writes
   including a change from an already-open page, preserved account/other
   memberships, and retained shared content and attribution identifiers.
   Distinguishes persistent author ID from the unresolved displayed name.
   Gives the actor a recognizable completion/denial outcome without requiring
   new infrastructure. Does not block all drafting pending the display decision,
   delete or reassign shared content, or expand this into whole-account deletion.
4. Actually updates only the PRD's departure-flow and departure-acceptance
   sections (3 and 4), replacing their placeholder content with a coherent
   bounded handoff. Preserves the title/preamble and sections 1, 2, 5, and 6
   verbatim, including headings and historical source wording; all other raw
   files remain byte-identical. No full-document redesign, appended competing
   current plan, duplicated editable policy document, code change, or separate
   user-facing update command. Internal links remain usable. Grade meaningful
   editing and scope together, not a change summary without the actual artifact.
5. Supplies observable planned acceptance scenarios for the agreed flow,
   continued shared records, organization isolation, and stale-screen access.
   For author-name display, keeps the expected outcome conditional on the
   unresolved decision instead of asserting both incompatible results pass.
   Retains V-01/V-02 as the original bounded v1.8 staging passes: they do not
   become failures or newly executed proof of v2 self-departure, stale saves,
   or name display. No product test, implementation, external research, or
   contact is performed or claimed. The whole edited PRD fits 600
   whitespace-delimited words; the final response briefly identifies changes
   and remaining questions rather than supplying an unrequested full plan.

## Source-based rationale and limits

`skills/bandit/references/evidence-and-decisions.md` requires equally
authoritative conflicts to remain explicit; timestamps alone do not resolve
them. `specification.md` and `changes.md` support narrow edits, continuation
of independent work, scoped supersession, and preservation of historical
verification conditions. The supplied documents make only one policy topic
incompatible; surrounding access and content behavior can still be specified.

Case 03 distinguishes adopted policy from an unadopted implementation-derived
document; sequence 12 supplies explicit adopted supersession. This case instead
tests unresolved adopted authority plus narrow artifact editing. It does not
measure deployed access security, actual privacy compliance, or organizational
approval. No external legal rule or preferred display policy is part of grading.
