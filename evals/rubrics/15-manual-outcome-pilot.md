# Manual outcome pilot development check

Written before the first recorded run. All operational observations and people
in this case are synthetic. This is a next-round author-designed development
probe, not a held-out benchmark, market evidence, or a round-3 result. The task
agent receives only the raw request/brief and selected instruction snapshot,
never this rubric or author diagnoses. Preserve the pre-run rubric digest and
full output before grading or changing criteria. No expected output is supplied.

## 15 — Manual outcome pilot

1. Proposes one bounded manual test that delivers usable final listing material
   to relevant shop owners, rather than replacing the requested outcome test
   with interviews, a landing page, or software construction. Connects intake,
   required factual information, drafting, owner clarification/revision, and
   final delivery; publication remains the owner's action. Defines what is
   included well enough for an owner to accept the offer and for the operator
   to know when an item is finished. Does not invent dimensions, condition, or
   price, promise sales, assume account access, or require forbidden visits.
2. Bounds the complete founder workload within six hours across six one-hour
   evening sessions, including a credible allowance for contact/setup, actual
   production, clarification/revision, delivery, and outcome/payment follow-up
   as applicable. Uses the small observed timing sample without presenting it
   as proven capacity: observed active totals are 20, 28, 50, and 21 minutes per
   item, excluding the stated overhead and elapsed reply waits. Accepts ranges,
   conservative estimates, staged admission, or another defensible provisional
   budget; does not require an exact number of shops/items or a particular
   statistic. Makes a feasible intake/complexity or workload limit and explains
   what is reduced, paused, or declined if work exceeds it. A setup-only budget
   followed by unbounded promised delivery does not pass.
3. Fits the two-week observation window and limited contact schedule, including
   possible slow replies and late or incomplete inputs. Gives a workable rule
   for missing facts, revisions, and turnaround rather than assuming immediate
   customer replies or founder availability outside the supplied sessions.
   Already accepted work has a communicated completion/re-scope path if a cap
   is reached; a capacity limit is not permission to silently abandon delivery.
   Keeps proposed cash spending within 50,000 KRW and does not assume free extra
   operators. No elaborate workflow or exact service-level target is required.
4. Identifies the decision-changing uncertainty and specifies an observable
   measure with relevant eligible people/items and an opportunity/window to act.
   Distinguishes delivered drafts, owner acceptance, actual use/publication,
   and payment where these bear the claim. Makes willingness to pay testable
   through clear proposed offer/price/commitment conditions, or explicitly
   leaves it unresolved if the chosen test is free. The suggested five-item
   price is not an existing package obligation or observed demand. Past thanks,
   expressed inconvenience, and a free delivery are not purchases; publication
   is not evidence of incremental sales. Tracks operator effort through the
   outcome, not only recruitment, to inform feasibility without claiming proven
   profitability or scalable automation economics.
5. States how plausible positive, negative, or incomplete observations would
   change the next development decision. Proposed thresholds are provisional
   and proportionate to a tiny accessible sample, not universal validation
   cutoffs. Avoids treating owners/items without a timely chance to receive or
   use the result as observed rejection, or brief usage as long-term retention.
   A paid or free test may pass; no vendor, price, sample size, or build/no-build
   conclusion is prescribed. Answers in Korean within 600 whitespace-delimited
   words and remains a proposal: no outreach, delivery, posting, payment, live
   experiment, external research, file changes, or implementation is performed
   or claimed.

## Source-based rationale and limits

`skills/bandit/references/research.md` calls for observable behavior, relevant
audience and exposure conditions, resource constraints, decision consequences,
and a manual-test budget through delivery rather than recruitment alone. Its
evidence distinctions separate value received, payment, retention, and profitable
delivery. This fixture transfers those requirements to a small manual content
service with variable active effort and customer-response delays.

Grade coherent meaning and feasibility, not the presence of a budget table or
checklist headings. The four-item convenience sample and simulated contacts
cannot validate real operator capacity, demand, a conversion rate, or the
commercial opportunity. This case only observes how a fresh task agent proposes
a test from the supplied facts; it does not execute that test.
