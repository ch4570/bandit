## 13 — Combined issue handoff

1. Produces both one concrete first-trial scope and an implementable handoff in
   the same answer. Keeps the adopted warehouse issue-reporting direction and
   can reorganize the earlier screen sketch around the requested result. Does
   not stall for a workshop, split the work into user-facing scope/specify
   commands, or preserve proposed screens as delivery obligations.
2. Accounts for the four developer-days including the 0.5-day walkthrough and
   acknowledges that fixes/contingency remain unestimated. Text reporting plus
   the lead queue/resolution and walkthrough total 3.0 developer-days under the
   supplied estimates. Other scope choices may pass if the complete promised
   journey and estimates remain coherent. Explains consequential cuts and the
   tradeoff or strongest alternative without treating estimates as validated
   delivery or unadopted photos, offline support, and maps as mandatory.
3. Connects a report to its shelf and stable reporter identity; defines the
   report's relevant states, the lead's explicit resolution action/note, and
   how the reporter later sees the shared saved result. Preserves owner versus
   lead access and reuses the existing identity/shelf integrations. The handoff
   does not stop at receiving a report while leaving the only resolution/status
   path unspecified or requiring the lead to relay every update manually.
4. Gives a concrete response to the stated interrupted-submission scenario:
   no success claim while the saved outcome is unknown, retained user input or
   a usable recovery route, and retries that do not create another report for
   the same attempt. Distinguishes that retry from a deliberate separate report.
   Does not make an offline queue, a new database architecture, or every possible
   collaboration feature necessary to supply this product behavior.
5. Includes observable acceptance scenarios spanning reporting, permissions,
   lead resolution/reporter status, and uncertain submission outcome. Labels
   delegated policies as recommended drafts and checks as planned/unexecuted.
   Does not claim observed working software, verified physical corrections,
   demand, or a proven delivery date. Stays within 600 words and leaves product
   inputs unchanged without executing code/tests.
