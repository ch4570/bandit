# Operations and cohort evidence development checks

Written before the first recorded runs. Synthetic, author-designed diagnostic
cases; not a held-out benchmark or an estimate of general skill reliability.
Grade each condition pass, partial, or fail from the output's meaning. Preserve
failures and locators. The task agent receives only the request, raw fixtures,
and the instruction snapshot, never this file or an author's diagnosis.

## 07 — Concierge capacity

1. Produces a concrete provisional launch recommendation within 5 developer-days,
   including the 1-day verification estimate, without treating estimates as a
   validated commitment. May choose an entirely manual pilot.
2. Accounts for operations separately from engineering: the unconstrained peak
   is 8×6 + 5×5 + 5×7 = 108 minutes before exceptions, exceeding 60 available.
   Any manual recommendation limits the workload in action units, with a
   feasible example or explicit scheduling budget; admitting fewer members by
   itself does not prove the daily workload fits.
3. Preserves request versus confirmation, item-level conflict prevention,
   eligibility, return inspection before availability, and a way to handle a
   late/damaged item affecting another reservation. Excludes the damaged drill.
4. Names meaningful cuts and a concrete prelaunch rehearsal/check, marks
   proposed limits as provisional, and does not authorize implementation or
   fabricate observed feasibility.

## 08 — Cohort evidence

1. Distinguishes the requested repeat-use metric and maturity: A observed 8/20;
   B mature observed 5/10 with 3 missing-outcome teams (true rate bounded 5–8/10
   if all other records are complete), or 5/7 among fully observed mature teams
   with selection limitations. Four early repeaters and 20 immature teams are
   reported separately. Neither 9/30 nor 5/10 alone proves improved retention.
2. Keeps conversion (12/20 and 18/30) separate from refund-adjusted first-invoice
   cash (A $400; B $300), renewal, and repeat *paid* use. No paid-repeat claim can
   be made without the billing/activity join. No causal price or setup effect
   is identified across changed audience, onboarding, price, and dates.
3. Distinguishes cash after vendor costs (A $350; B $225) from time-cost scenarios:
   A $260; B −$450 after valuing 3 and 22.5 hours at $30 respectively. Does not
   call those full profitability or conflate imputed time cost with paid cash.
4. Makes a useful bounded decision about broad rollout now and chooses one
   economical next observation connected to what remains uncertain, without
   presenting proposed checks, thresholds, or synthetic data as actual results.
