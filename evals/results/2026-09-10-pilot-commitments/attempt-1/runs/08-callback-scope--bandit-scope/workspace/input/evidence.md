# Synthetic callback evidence, recorded 2026-09-09

The invented product RelayBoard serves field-service dispatch desks. This is a convenience
sample of four existing customer desks, not a representative market study.

In a 14-day manual review, the desks recorded 240 missed inbound calls with a promised
callback. Sixty-two callbacks happened more than 12 hours after the promised time. Staff
assigned one primary reason to each late case: 31 were lost during shift handoff, 17 had
no named owner, and 14 were waiting for missing customer contact details. The review did
not establish that software changes would prevent these delays.

Eight dispatchers were interviewed. Five asked to see outstanding callbacks when starting
a shift, two asked for automatic text messages, and one asked for a weekly manager report.
They were not shown a working prototype. Managers did not agree on a willingness-to-pay
number. Only 116 of the 240 call records had an auditable stored SMS-consent record.

Two of the sampled desks each have 3–5 dispatchers, one time zone, and one shared phone
number. Their owner field is populated for 91% of callbacks. The other two desks each have
over 30 dispatchers across several branches; transferred cases can temporarily have two
owners and different branch time zones. No multi-branch ownership study has been run.

The current system records promised time, assigned dispatcher, branch, completion time,
and optional contact details. It does not record which person saw a reminder. Existing
server events can measure callback completion and ownership changes. A new email vendor
or analytics warehouse is not available during the pilot.
