# Synthetic decision and operating constraints

We can spend one afternoon learning before choosing whether to devote our next two weekends to this idea. We could explore location naming, return habits, or something else the evidence supports. We have no commitment to building an app.

Team member Dev wrote: “The poll proves theatres want this. Every amateur company would pay £25 a month, and clearer labels should halve delays.” This is Dev’s belief. There have been no payment offers, purchasing conversations, observed trials, or measurements of delay reduction.

Lantern Hall has already agreed to one optional research slot next Tuesday: twenty minutes before rehearsal, plus five minutes for wrap-up. Six adult volunteers have opted into that slot: three familiar with this store room and three who have visited at most once. Attendance is not guaranteed. We cannot recruit anyone else or schedule a second session this week. Mira and Dev can both attend; together they have two preparation hours and can spend at most £20 on ordinary printing and stationery.

The venue permits a tabletop exercise with six retired props, a copy of the current shelf list, and removable paper cards or inserts. The retired props are clearly separated from production stock. We may not hide, move, or relabel production props, alter the room’s permanent signs, interrupt rehearsal, or create a real missing-prop incident. Internet access is unreliable. No software development is available for this test. Handwritten observations are permitted with participants’ agreement; no photography, audio recording, or personal identifiers in the notes.

No outcome data from this slot exists. We want to use it to reduce one important uncertainty. A small exercise here will not automatically demonstrate performance-night benefits or adoption at other venues.
