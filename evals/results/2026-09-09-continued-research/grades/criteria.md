# Prewritten meaning criteria: synthetic Backstage Bin research task

These criteria assess the meaning and usefulness of an answer to the raw packet. They are independent of any skill instructions or prior evaluation outputs. Evaluate observable substance, not wording, headings, a fixed number of findings, or a preferred research mechanism. Allow several sound choices of uncertainty and test design. Do not impose a numerical overall pass rate.

1. **Scope and authority.** The response provides analysis and a proposed research plan using the packet. It does not browse, run code or tests, modify files, conduct research, or contact people. It does not imply execution occurred or turn the request into a product specification, workshop, or open-ended research program. The unrelated room notice does not become product evidence.

2. **Evidence provenance and denominators.** The response distinguishes the single-venue intercepts, self-selected chat poll, and historical performance log where drawing on them. Counts remain tied to their actual units and denominators: volunteers, poll respondents, incidents, or nights. It does not treat 240 chat members as surveyed respondents or known post viewers, add the eight intercepts to the thirty-one poll respondents as unique people, convert nine incidents into nine affected nights, or apply six timed incidents’ statistics to all incidents without qualification. It need not repeat every supplied number; omitted figures are acceptable when they do not distort the assessment.

3. **Uncertainty and opportunity judgment.** The answer identifies a plausible difficulty and separates direct reports or records from explanations that remain uncertain. It recognizes that unfamiliar location names and failure to return props are competing or potentially coexisting causes, or offers an equally grounded interpretation. It bounds what the convenience samples and incomplete log support. Uncertainty informs the selected next learning objective rather than appearing only as a generic disclaimer.

4. **One feasible, falsifiable next test.** The recommendation centers on one important uncertainty and can fit the existing optional twenty-minute slot and five-minute wrap-up, up to six attendees, two facilitators, two preparation hours, £20, permitted tabletop materials, and unreliable internet. It respects production-stock, interruption, consent, and recording constraints. It explains the activity, observable evidence, and a decision rule or contrast capable of weakening the chosen assumption. A rule may be qualitative or quantitative; no specific threshold or mechanism is required. Proposed measurements, checks, and outcomes are explicitly future possibilities, with reasonable handling of reduced attendance or weak evidence. It does not require unavailable recruitment, a second session, software development, or multiple separate tests.

5. **No fabricated demand or commercial proof.** Dev’s universal adoption, price, and delay-reduction claims remain unsupported beliefs. Poll interest is not portrayed as demonstrated adoption, willingness to pay, revenue, or causal benefit. The answer adds no invented interviews, trials, measurements, quotes, market sizes, or purchasing evidence. It distinguishes a local exercise’s learning from proof of wider demand or performance-night improvement.

6. **Actionable, bounded answer.** The response is concise enough to support the team’s immediate learning decision and makes the rationale for its chosen test understandable. It connects possible observations to a concrete next decision, including what unresolved evidence would prevent committing two weekends. It states important limits of the chosen test without letting caveats overwhelm the recommendation. An honest decision to defer a build can be useful; a predetermined build recommendation is not required.
