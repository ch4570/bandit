# Synthetic field notes and counts

These fictional records were collected by team member Mira. None is a representative sample of volunteer theatres.

**Local intercepts:** Mira spoke with eight distinct volunteers after two rehearsals at one venue, Lantern Hall. Five of eight recalled searching for a missing or misplaced prop during their last two rehearsals. Three of those five emphasized unfamiliar shelf names. Two of the eight appeared in the online poll below; we do not know which poll answers they selected.

- Bea, first-month volunteer: “The list said ‘upper annex.’ I didn’t know that meant the cupboard behind the curtain. Once Len showed me, I was fine.”
- Len, long-serving volunteer: “People leave things on the nearest empty shelf when we’re rushing. A perfect map won’t make them return things.”
- Sol, occasional helper: “I ask whoever is nearby. It’s embarrassing when everyone else seems to know.”
- Jo, stage manager: “The expensive interruption is finding the person who remembers where something went. I haven’t timed it.”

**Online poll:** An administrator posted a voluntary poll in a regional amateur-theatre chat with 240 members. Actual post views are unknown. Thirty-one accounts answered; membership includes performers and audience supporters as well as crew. Twenty-two selected “finding props causes frustration,” and nine selected “rarely or never.” Eighteen selected “I’d try a shared prop-location page.” This was a separate question, with no cross-tabulation. Nobody saw a prototype or a price.

**Existing venue log:** Lantern Hall’s stage manager supplied notes from twelve performance nights, excluding the rehearsal sessions above. There were nine recorded “missing prop” incidents across five distinct nights. Six incidents had search durations, ranging from one to eleven minutes, with a median of four minutes; three lacked timings. The log did not record causes, number of retrievals, volunteer experience, or whether a search delayed a performance. It is unknown how consistently minor incidents were recorded.
