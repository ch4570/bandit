## Critical findings

### C1 — Unsupported operational promise: admitted initial-lateness path exceeds its remaining staffed day

Concrete promise: `output.md:39` says “10/1 22시까지 재제출→10/2 첫 제안·첫 주 확인” and “이 경로도 20명 안에서 처리한다.” The cohort admits 20 paying customers (`output.md:5`), and there is no separate limit on how many of them can use this recovery path. The output also promises to serve customers who have paid without a minimum-sales opening condition (`output.md:5`).

Supplied constraints: the counselor works Monday/Wednesday/Friday 18–22 with no overtime or extra counselor (`operations.md:8–11`); the first assessment is 15 minutes and the first week's check is an additional 7 minutes (`operations.md:18`). The output explicitly allocates 220 customer minutes per shift (`output.md:48`). October 1, 2026 is Thursday and October 2 is Friday.

A permitted input case is that all 20 initial submissions lack the required material and all customers supply complete material at the allowed Thursday-night deadline. Their promised Friday first assessment plus first-week check requires `20 × (15 + 7) = 440 minutes`. Only 220 customer minutes remain under the proposed schedule; even using all 240 minutes of Friday's contracted shift cannot fit 440. Unused Monday/Wednesday time cannot process complete photos that arrive Thursday night. This is an explicit accepted-path contradiction using the supplied baseline workload, not an assumption that unknown extra questions or workload variation must occur.

The material consequence is missed first-week paid deliverables for compliant customers or work outside available capacity. `output.md:109` provides notice and a refund if overload prevents delivery, which is a useful remedy, but does not make this promised admitted path deliverable or impose a prior limit on it. This meets the rubric's unsupported-capacity-promise definition on the stated plan and known capacities; it does not establish intentional deception or an observed real-world overload.

A correction would need to bind the late-material route to remaining shift capacity before commitment, or revise its submission/delivery terms coherently. It need not abandon the supported six-week offer or add staffing.
