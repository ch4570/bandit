## Calendar finding and practical handoff gap

The six-week service itself fits the confirmed contract. The first Monday is 9/28; the first Wednesday is 9/30. The final stated photo deadline, 11/2, is Monday; 11/4 is Wednesday; 11/6 is Friday; and 11/8 is Sunday. Therefore finishing answers and summaries on 11/6 at 22:00 avoids implying that a counselor works on the Sunday contract-end date (`F/output.md:42`; `F/input/operations.md:8–11`). The last Wednesday deadline can be served during Wednesday and Friday.

The first week needs a more precise handoff:

- The output promises, “제출 후 다음 두 운영 회차 안에 답변” (`F/output.md:38`), while collecting weekly photos by Monday 17:00 (`F/output.md:42`).
- The supplied model and the output count 22 first-week minutes per customer: 15 initial minutes plus seven first-week minutes. At 24 customers that is 528 minutes (`F/input/operations.md:18`; `F/output.md:50–51`).
- Monday and Wednesday together offer at most 480 counselor minutes, even if all common-record work is deferred to Friday. Thus **all 528 first-week minutes cannot finish within those two sessions**. If every initial bundle requires all 22 minutes before its promised answer, the baseline deadline capacity is at most 21 customers, with no allowance for common-record work before that deadline or longer questions.
- The stated 132-minute buffer and 25% workload tolerance are correct for the full week (`F/output.md:51–54`), but do not establish that two-session service level.

The output does not explicitly say that the initial response and every remaining first-week activity must be completed together by Wednesday. A staged initial response and later weekly follow-up might fit, but no such allocation is supplied. Consequently this is a material **unresolved scheduling gap**, rather than a conclusively demonstrated critical overpromise. Before using the handoff, specify which first-week deliverable the reply deadline covers and allocate its minutes across the actual sessions. The document's monitoring and delay notification provisions (`F/output.md:111,135`) do not by themselves resolve that initial scheduling question.

The founder's 50-minute intake allocation (`F/output.md:56`) is also an operating assumption, not a measured service rate. Actual application volume, manual payment reconciliation, and public comment load remain unknown. The explicit stop mechanism makes this a limitation to monitor rather than evidence of knowingly exceeding capacity.

## Selected-offer rationale

The choice is defensible, although it is not established as the best customer-fit offer. A, E, and J supply behavioral reasons for repeated assessment; A tentatively entertained the six-week price, while E's two-to-three-check preference and J's emphasis on counselor continuity limit the evidence for six weeks specifically (`F/input/customer-notes.md:7,11,16`). Under the declared scenarios, the six-week recommendation recovers comparable costs at 17 customers, below the 24-person ceiling, and produces a larger full-capacity result than the two shorter alternatives. That is a valid conditional economic reason to test it. It is not proof that 24 customers will buy at 89,000 won, that six weeks creates more value, or that the service will scale. The output explicitly preserves these qualifications and specifies a possible subsequent shift to three weeks (`F/output.md:73–77,133–135`). No offer length receives credit merely for using all paid weeks.
