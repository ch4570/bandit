# Metric-reporting audit before a skill change

Round 3 repeatedly received a partial on case 02 criterion 3. Independent
read-only inspection of the original inputs, outputs and grading history
finds omitted explicit comparisons, not demonstrated arithmetic confusion or
an incorrect causal claim. This distinction changes the next action: a skill
edit is not justified by that partial alone.

The [raw report](../../../cases/02-offer-change/observations.md) contains four
repeat users among six senders with a later opportunity, fourteen senders in
total, and twenty-four registrants. The corresponding views are 4/6 (66.7%)
opportunity-conditioned repeat use, 4/14 (28.6%) observed repeat-user share
among senders, and 4/24 (16.7%) among registrants. The broader shares are not
opportunity-adjusted retention: eight senders had no later opportunity yet,
and ten non-senders' opportunity status was uncollected.

The round-3 skill outputs preserve these counts and conditions, report 4/6
correctly, and reject causal attribution across changed price, sharing,
recruitment and observation windows. They omit the two broader repeat shares.
The baseline additionally distinguishes 4/14 from the opportunity rate but
does not supply the 4/24 view. Their 14/24 is sending conversion, not repeat
use. The [existing grades](../../2026-09-09-overnight-round3/grades.md) already
state that these are omitted comparisons rather than arithmetic errors.

The [frozen rubric](../../../RUBRIC.md) names all three perspectives. Its
established explicit-comparison interpretation supports the historical
partials. The [original grader](../../2026-09-07/grades-02-03-05.md) also
disclosed that “separates” could instead mean avoiding conflation. The raw
request does not explicitly demand all three ratios. We do not change the
rubric, reinterpret old outputs as passes, or turn that reporting preference
into a universal skill requirement.

Fresh round-7 case-02 executions retain the original request and criterion
interpretation. A separate new domain case is authored with its own rubric
before execution, tying required metric distinctions to its actual rollout
decision. Clear counts and prose can establish those meanings without an
arbitrary required table or ratio inventory. These are development checks,
not a held-out comparison or proof that a skill change is needed.

This audit read local synthetic fixtures and historical records only. It did
not modify instructions, source evidence, previous scores, or historical files.
