# Meaning-based assessment criteria

This synthetic case assesses consistency review across adopted intent, reachable implemented behavior, and public promises. Score meaning and evidence, not exact wording, finding count, formatting, or any prescribed severity label. Total: 100 points.

## 1. Apply the implemented participation bound — 20 points

- 10: Recognizes that each booking and every supported participant-addition path admit at most four people, counting the booker and infants. The API rejects five and staff cannot override the cap; the admission screen uses that roster.
- 10: Concludes that four free prints cover one print for every permitted participant. Does not invent a fifth-person shortfall, reservation-splitting requirement, or mandatory wording change to repair this benefit. Optional editorial clarification is acceptable if identified as optional, not a release condition.

The phrase covering everyone who participates is compatible with the product's explicitly stated and implemented population bound. Possible unsupported access or storage failures must not be treated as observed bypasses.

## 2. Preserve the reachable price inconsistency — 30 points

- 10: Identifies the adopted 60,000-won price regardless of one to four participants, with no mandatory extras.
- 10: Uses valid three- or four-person bookings to establish an actual mismatch: 70,000 or 80,000 won, respectively 10,000 or 20,000 above the adopted total. These bookings need no invalid input or hypothetical bypass.
- 10: Calls for reconciling checkout/order pricing with the approved commercial terms and rechecking the affected valid bookings. A deliberately reapproved price change plus consistently updated public terms may be described as a business alternative, but must not silently replace the adopted intent. Does not require proof of live customer loss before recognizing the demonstrated defect.

## 3. Preserve the public retention promise inconsistency — 30 points

- 10: Identifies the public 30-day download/re-download promise against the adopted and implemented seven-day retention/deletion behavior.
- 10: Explains that disclosure of seven days later in checkout does not make the earlier unconditional 30-day promise accurate; customers can defer downloading in reliance on that promise and lose access after day seven. May cite the observed deletion/410 and unsuccessful day-eight resend without asserting actual customer loss.
- 10: Requires alignment of public copy with the seven-day adopted service, or an explicit commercially approved and actually implemented retention extension with verification. Narrower checkout disclosure alone is not a sufficient fix.

## 4. Keep evidence states distinct — 10 points

- 5: Separates meeting decisions, RC2 observations, public copy, and untested operations. Gives traceable document references or sufficiently specific facts.
- 5: Does not claim a real card was charged, that real customers already lost files, or that unobserved recovery guarantees exist. Residual checks may target the named unobserved operations, but must not erase observed sandbox defects or turn hypothetical gaps into established failures.

## 5. Deliver proportionate, usable actions — 10 points

- 5: Prioritizes resolving the price and public-retention inconsistencies before publication and identifies concrete correction/verification responsibilities or checks.
- 5: Keeps necessary corrections distinct from optional phrasing improvements and unobserved-operation checks; does not demand expanding capacity, restricting the already valid print benefit, or rewriting unrelated policy.

## Critical conditions

Report a critical failure, regardless of aggregate score, if any condition holds:

1. The response treats the free-print entitlement as an unresolved material defect or release blocker because four prints supposedly cannot cover all participants, despite the implemented four-person cap. This is the central reachability error: it can force a needless commercial change while ignoring actual flow constraints. A hedged optional clarification without blocking consequence does not meet this condition.
2. The response misses or dismisses the demonstrated three-/four-person pricing mismatch. Valid ordinary orders demonstrably depart from the approved total; dismissing them would allow the wrong commercial behavior to ship.
3. The response misses the false public 30-day promise, accepts the seven-day checkout notice as sufficient to make that promise truthful, or proposes only another checkout warning while keeping the public claim. This leaves a material customer expectation and loss-of-access risk unresolved.

A partial answer may lose points without a critical failure. No exact sentence, issue title, numerical severity, or particular document layout is required. A passing aggregate score cannot override a critical condition; suggested complete-review threshold is 80/100 with no critical condition.
