# Case 12 — connected first paid launch plan

Source of truth: the Korean request and accompanying synthetic records in `evals/cases/12-launch-handoff/`. These criteria concern an answer-only task. Read all raw files before grading; the facts below are grading anchors, not a prescribed answer.

## P0 criteria

### J12-1 — Research changes a decision

The answer distinguishes observed behavior, interview statements, and unknowns. It preserves that six interviewees represent four cafés, the manual pilot used three cafés for free, reminders were assisted, and paid customers are zero. It need not repeat every count, but any counts/rates it uses must have the right unit and denominator. For example, there were 28 memo-present opportunities out of 42 and 20 read-marked opportunities out of those 28; 20/42 answers a different question and is acceptable if labeled accordingly.

It identifies an unresolved question consequential to the proposed launch, with a feasible way to answer it. It does not call the pilot causal proof of reduced errors, validated demand, market representativeness, or established willingness to pay. It does not invent competitor or market facts. A short, correctly qualified synthesis passes; another extensive research program is not required.

### J12-2 — A connected target, offer, and scope

The proposal selects an initial segment and problem and connects them to the supplied records. It distinguishes the employee who writes/reads from the owner who buys or approves. It identifies a concrete first offer and ties research uncertainties to scope or launch decisions. The user must be able to tell what the team will provide first and what is deferred.

The plan fits single stores of at most 12 employees and at most 5 concurrently supported stores. It does not depend on building excluded payroll/POS/multi-store/employee-ranking functions or require employees to monitor personal devices off shift. A manual or lightweight web offer can pass. The grader must not require one specific target café, feature order, or technology.

### J12-3 — Marketing is usable and testable

The answer supplies customer-facing wording and a concrete CTA compatible with the proposed offer, evidence, and buying role. It chooses at least one realistically reachable channel from the records, or clearly labels a newly proposed route and its access dependency. Community permission, introductions, and the unknown audience of the employee video account cannot be treated as completed access or known buyer reach.

It specifies a small experiment with a decision-relevant hypothesis, a bounded audience/window, an observable signal with its counting unit, and an advance rule for continuing, changing, or stopping. It connects that rule to an actual next decision. A sequential offer test or an operational pilot is acceptable; randomized A/B testing and significance calculations are not required for this small pool. Thresholds can be proposed assumptions. Likes or interview compliments alone cannot be presented as evidence that owners will pay. Planned execution must be distinguishable from already obtained results.

### J12-4 — The UX handoff lets another person design the service

The answer gives a concrete employee-to-next-shift-to-owner flow and sufficient screen/content/state requirements for a designer to sketch it without reconstructing the product policy. At minimum it conveys what is recorded, what the next employee does, what the owner can see, and what the CTA or acknowledgement means. It preserves the observed distinction between reading a note and completing its work.

It covers first use or empty state plus an unresolved handoff such as a note not acknowledged, failed save, or missed next-shift access, with an understandable next action. Not every technical exception is required, and fabricated permissions architecture is not rewarded. The flow, wording, availability, and recovery path must be feasible under the selected manual/web delivery approach and limited team capacity. A list of screen names alone is partial.

### J12-5 — Business models have interpretable economics

The answer compares at least two materially distinguishable commercial arrangements: payer, charging unit/timing, included value, and an adoption or operating tradeoff. Different prices can count if the commercial structure changes, such as a setup fee plus recurring service versus an all-inclusive recurring fee; merely listing two unexplained prices does not. It makes one provisional choice and names evidence or an economic threshold that could change it.

For the recommended offer, calculations are transparent enough to reproduce and reflect the supplied 3.3% collection fee, 2,000 per store-month, 30 support minutes at 25,000 per hour, the 60,000 monthly fixed cost, and the additional one-time 40-minute onboarding work. An answer may alter a cost assumption with an explicit rationale and a measurement plan, but cannot silently delete it. It distinguishes a per-store contribution before fixed costs from overall profit, and first-cohort/onboarding economics from recurring economics. It does not treat unpaid founder labor as permanently free or extrapolate unmeasured LTV/CAC as known.

Calibration example, not required pricing: at 29,000 per store-month, the pre-fixed-cost recurring contribution is 29,000 − 957 − 2,000 − 12,500 = 13,543. Five recurring stores contribute 67,715 and leave 7,715 after the 60,000 fixed cost, before other unmeasured costs. Each new store additionally uses approximately 16,667 in onboarding labor. Rounding is acceptable; an arithmetically positive recurring illustration must not be described as proof that the first cohort or whole business is profitable. Other price points/models are welcome when period and cost units are consistent.

### J12-6 — The user can start a feasible four-week plan

The output connects the research, scope, marketing, design, and offer work in an ordered plan with owners and observable decision points. It budgets work well enough to assess feasibility against founder 10 hours per week/40 total, developer 28 total, designer 8 total, and 800,000 cash with 100,000 already reserved for tools. It includes onboarding/support effort for the number of stores proposed; it does not double-charge the reserved tools and the included monthly fixed cost.

Precision to every minute is not required: conservative allocations and explicit reserve can pass. An itemized total that exceeds a stated cap without reducing scope fails. An uncosted wish list or five disconnected essays is partial. The answer supplies the requested work now while labeling remaining decisions, and does not claim to have contacted, published, charged, built, or run an experiment. Workspace integrity is assessed separately from the saved answer.

## P1 diagnostic criterion

### J12-7 — Useful uncertainty reduction without unnecessary burden

The answer notices a useful source limitation beyond a generic “small sample” caveat, such as overlapping people/stores, assisted reminders without cross-tabs, or the absent buyer perspective at D. It uses that limitation to improve the next observation or recruitment choice. It names a lightweight way to track real support effort or to detect when a no-code/manual delivery assumption fails. Do not require broader recruitment or automation if it would violate the small experiment's purpose.

## Grading exclusions

Do not demand named frameworks, a full PRD, a market-size estimate, statistical confidence, polished design assets, specific colors, a new identity system, outreach execution, or a particular price. The raw request does not authorize those tasks. Missing headings are not missing substance; verify the actual reasoning and handoff.
