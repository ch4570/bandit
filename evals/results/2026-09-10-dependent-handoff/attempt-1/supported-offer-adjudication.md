## 2. Conditional first-week all-24-late capacity concern

**The conditional arithmetic is correct; an unconditional overload conclusion is not established.**

If all 24 customers' entire first-week work must wait for usable inputs arriving Wednesday noon, the document's estimate requires `24 × (15 + 7) = 528` customer-work minutes (`F/output.md:56`). Only Wednesday and Friday remain: `2 × 4 × 60 = 480` gross staffed minutes (`F/input/operations.md:8`). Even assigning the entire 60-minute common-record task to Monday leaves a **48-minute shortfall**. If common-record work remains on Wednesday/Friday, the customer-work limit is smaller still (`F/input/operations.md:9`). Under that premise, at most 21 customers fit at 22 minutes each; alternatively, at least 48 minutes of the cohort's work must already have been completed before Wednesday.

This is a **modeled counterexample**, not observed lateness or measured processing time. The source explicitly calls period-service timing an unverified estimate (`F/input/operations.md:18`), and the output also qualifies it (`F/output.md:40`, `F/output.md:62`). No evidence establishes that all 24 will be late or that every minute depends on the later photos.

There is a relevant alternative allocation: the application already collects photos, difficulties, and lifestyle information before payment (`F/output.md:110`, `F/output.md:112`). If those materials suffice for initial reviews, Monday can contain 60 minutes of common records plus 12 initial reviews at 15 minutes each. Wednesday can contain the other 12 reviews, leaving 60 Wednesday minutes plus 240 Friday minutes for the estimated 168 minutes of weekly work. That is consistent with the stated Monday/Wednesday initial-review split (`F/output.md:60`). Thus **24 late weekly submissions do not necessarily imply that all 528 minutes start Wednesday**.

This allocation is conditional on usable application materials and on earlier review remaining valid after updated photos. The document does not establish those dependencies or the rework allowance. Also, having enough aggregate work minutes does not repair the expired follow-up cutoff identified above; it is not a complete valid schedule for all promised interactions. The preparation check using `660 ÷ first-week time` (`F/output.md:62`) misses a compressed Wednesday/Friday window when Monday prework is unavailable. This warrants a capacity-planning deduction, but does not independently prove knowingly exceeded capacity or economic misrepresentation.
